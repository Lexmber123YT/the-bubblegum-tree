The Bubblegum Tree

A boy, his grandpa, a dog named Pixie, and a tree that grows gumballs. Written by Brenda Oney Morris and published by Christian Faith Publishing.

The story

Troy is learning to work the garden with Grandpa: how to plant, how to water, how to wait. One afternoon, helping out in the shed, he finds a small, colorful box. Grandpa tells him what it is. A bubblegum tree. His own dad gave it to him years ago. "Let's give it a shot."

They plant it together, with Pixie the dog keeping watch. Then Troy waits. And waits. Grandpa reminds him that everything grows in its own season, and that some things you have to believe in before you can see them.

When the gumballs finally come, Troy's mind is blown. He sets up a stand and sells them by the bucket. Everybody loves them. But when he points to the tree, the other kids laugh, and a grown-up shakes his head. Troy knows what he saw.

Then one morning, the gumballs are gone. The tree is still there, tall and green, and it turns out to be just right for a treehouse. Some seasons end so a new one can begin.

What Troy learns

  • Patience A tree takes its time. So do most good things.
  • Faith You can plant a seed and trust it will grow, even when nobody else believes you.
  • Grace Grandpa gets down on one knee to talk to Troy eye to eye. That's where grace is found, he says. Down on your knees.
  • Seasons change Good things come and go. Beauty is still there afterward, just a different kind.
  • Family The people who came before you have a lot to teach, if you'll sit on the bucket and listen.

Where the idea came from

Brenda's dad was a gardener. He taught her six kids how to plant, and on summer mornings he'd sit them on a bucket with a pair of tweezers to pick squash bugs off the squash. One day Brenda and one of her sons were turning a quarter gumball over in their hands and wondered out loud: what if gumballs grew on trees? The thought sat for a few years. Then it became this book.

Grandpa in the story is drawn from Brenda's father, and a little from her oldest son. Her dad grew up poor, ran a grocery store, and never let anyone leave his house without fruit, a jar of honey, or a meal. He passed away in 2026, a few months before the book came out.

And yes, Pixie the little white pit bull is the family dog. She had to be in it.
